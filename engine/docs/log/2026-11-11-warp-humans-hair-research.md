# Hair rendering references for Warp Humans

11 November 2026. This investigation follows the close-ups of Growing Guns on Mancave, especially the bob on player two in match seed 1. The rejected images show regular dots across the eye and a horizontal line across the forehead. The local experiment generates curves over an outer surface sampled from a fitted haircut. One iteration also raised tips to a common brow height. That operation can flatten several projected curves onto the same line. Neither adding strands nor softening that line would define where the hairs actually grow.

## What the game developers describe

**Alan Wake 2.** Remedy lead character artist John Crossland describes styling Melanie Liburd's hair, testing styles and observing motion with a hair double. The team matched the strands to her hair and styled them into Saga's groom. Secondary motion was an explicit visual goal. Crossland and Little Red Zombies also credit gameplay look development and final tweaks. Remedy documents GPU mesh shaders for scene rendering and moment-based order-independent transparency for fog and other transparent surfaces. Those sources leave the exact runtime representation of Saga's hair open. [Remedy developer AMA](https://www.alanwake.com/story/the-dev-teams-first-ama-best-questions-and-answers/) · [Crossland's Saga breakdown](https://whatjohndraws.artstation.com/projects/XgVmZL) · [Remedy on Northlight](https://www.remedygames.com/article/how-northlight-makes-alan-wake-2-shine)

**Grand Theft Auto VI.** Rockstar's second trailer is in-game PlayStation 5 footage. It provides a reference for following a character's silhouette and loose hair through a head turn. I found no Rockstar technical breakdown identifying the runtime strand primitive, transparency algorithm, texture use or simulation budget. Inferring any of these from compression and motion in the trailer would give the Warp implementation a false technical premise. [Official trailer and capture description](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VQRLujxTm3c) · [Rockstar media](https://www.rockstargames.com/VI/downloads/videos)

**Dragon Age: The Veilguard.** EA reports 50,000 strands per character, over 100 hairstyles, and separate simulation and render tessellation. Frostbite rasterizes hair with a custom compute pipeline. BioWare describes changes to light transmission and shadowing, and two stages of compositing to handle transparent effects and fog. An earlier Frostbite presentation illustrates the importance of measuring visibility and coverage before expensive shading. Its specific MSAA implementation belongs to that earlier presentation. [EA and BioWare's production breakdown](https://www.ea.com/ea-studios/motive/news/strand-hair-dragon-age-the-veilguard) · [Frostbite rendering slides](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2019/hair_presentation_final.pdf)

## Asset size, movement and distance

Bhokare and colleagues store a volumetric control mesh with a root layer on the scalp and generate strands inside its extrusions on the GPU. A stable local frame attaches procedural variations to a deforming hairstyle. Their 100-character scene used 13–21 KB per model, 1.7 MB total, and about 2 ms to rasterize the hair on a 4090 with LOD and 8× MSAA. This measures rasterization in that demonstration. A full stadium frame also includes animation, lighting, shadows and compositing. Their project page includes an interactive demonstration and information about patents on their specific hair-mesh representation. [SIGGRAPH 2024 project and demonstration](https://cemyuksel.com/research/hairmesh_rendering/)

Hsu and colleagues show 128 simulated guides driving 106,000 rendered hairs in one example. Their measured 0.34 ms is the interpolation step. They show why naïve interpolation stretches curls when neighbouring guides deform differently. Epic's groom documentation gives a production example of selecting strand, card or mesh representations per LOD. Together these suggest separate controls for the amount of simulation, the number of rendered fibres and the distant silhouette. [Physically guided hair interpolation](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/physically-guided-hair-interpolation/hairinterp-siggraph2024.pdf) · [Epic groom scalability](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/groom-scalability-and-performance-with-unreal-engine)

## Proposed next experiment

1. Fit one side-parted bob to the actual scalp. Mark the part, temples, hairline and the direction a lock leaves its root. Store roots in triangle coordinates so they follow the same facial deformation as the head. Give fringe, sides and crown independent guide curves. Use reference photographs at the front, three quarters and in profile to check the exposed forehead and hair mass before adding flyaways.
2. Keep the per-person data to a seed and optional style constraints. Distribute roots over the scalp by area with a stable density falloff at the hairline. Generate hairs around the guides on the GPU. Vary root position, length, curl and tip separation coherently within a lock. Preserve the same roots as detail increases. Drive secondary movement from a smaller set of guides with inertia, damping and head contact.
3. Measure subpixel coverage and transmittance under camera movement. Rotate the light to check that the highlight follows the fibres. Match the near and distant silhouettes derived from the same hairstyle. Use the head's projected pixel height independently in each split-screen view.
4. Reuse match seed 1 at noon with fixed camera positions. Capture a slow orbit and a fast head turn, with temporal antialiasing on and off. Zoom out into the stadium, then repeat under the roof and at night. Inspect the forehead for rows, shelves and flicker, the temples for floating ends, and the silhouette through the detail transition. Measure GPU time separately for a single close head and a full split-screen round, reporting resolution, visible close heads and GPU p50/p95.

The first visual decision is whether one bob still reads as the same believable haircut from three angles and in motion. A wider seed sweep makes sense once that is true.
